# COCO workflow and collaboration evaluation — v2

## What this evaluation establishes

**Current COCO made the agreed procedure understandable and consistent in all
five observed handoffs; a fresh worker recovered the agreement and completed the
remaining work in all five. All five beginner advice cases supplied an appropriate,
usable next step.** These are operational workflow outcomes, rather than a claim
that COCO raises the model's underlying coding ability.

Procedure clarity, common completion boundaries, usable decision records and a
shared way to ask for help are valid team requirements. Meeting those requirements
is useful even when another approach can also meet them. This report evaluates
that behavior directly; it does not make faster implementation a prerequisite
for workflow value.

Against published 0.2.0, the observed differences were one additional-input handoff
and one advice response with an extra Jira prerequisite. Against no provided skill
bodies, the requested workflow outcomes were tied in these scenarios. COCO supplied
its explicit four-stage vocabulary and procedure map; the ordinary agent also
produced usable records when directly asked to prepare a handoff. **The evidence
supports COCO as a working, shared procedure package; it does not establish that
these tasks require it or that real humans learn it faster.**

The [original report](report.md) remains unchanged. Its function/cost comparison
answers a different question and is not reinterpreted as a test of team usability.

## How the scenarios tested the procedure

**Handoff:** the writer received the settled business choices in its conversation,
implemented and committed only import/list, and chose records for a colleague.
The experimenter did not insert those policy values into the seed files or write
a memory packet. After the writer ended, the successor received only the surviving
repository and a generic continuation request, in a new native thread. It had to
recover decisions, identify completed/pending work, implement summary and preserve
the completed import behavior. Actual CLI processes and persistent SQLite data
tested independent values and required error boundaries. Observers ran on copies
and left the successor's original files unchanged.

**Selection:** a newcomer asked what help/action to use next, why, and what result
to expect. The contexts were unsettled requirements, a ready specification/plan,
an unexpected CLI failure, committed work awaiting review, and browser acceptance
blocked by missing runtime. Advice had to remain read-only. An equivalent concrete
action in the no-body condition could receive full credit without COCO names.

All implementation handoffs had the same explicit automatic technical authority,
no-Jira convention, accepted same-agent inspection limitation, and publication
boundary. The successor received that technical authority again; this does **not**
test cross-chat consent persistence. Selection was a separate first-use advice
profile, not an automatic implementation run. Manual switching, specialist
preferences, open-ended grilling and real release execution were outside v2.

Each applicable subitem was graded 0–2 using the frozen observable rubric. A full
case requires every applicable subitem on that axis to be 2. Counts below are
observations out of five, not estimated population success rates. No combined
cross-axis quality score is used.

| Criterion | Current COCO | Published 0.2.0 | No provided skill bodies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clear agreement, completed/pending work and next safe action | 5/5 | 5/5 | 5/5 |
| Decisions, work checkpoint and truthful authority/completion remain consistent | 5/5 | 5/5 | 5/5 |
| Correct agreement recovery with usable source references | 5/5 | 5/5 | 5/5 |

All 15 writers stopped at the requested import-only checkpoint with working
import and without implementing summary early. All 15 successors preserved the
observed import/data boundaries. No successor was given owner rescue facts.

### Procedure clarity and consistency

The current procedure produced records from which the successor could recover
the agreement, completed slice, remaining summary and next permitted action in
every sample. The saved checkpoint was usable: the successor acted on it and the
result passed independent public probes. This goes beyond merely counting files.

All five current replies named the four-stage requirements → implementation →
review/verification → release framework. Four displayed the ten-skill overview
table; the ready-task reply recommended build directly and summarized the stages
in one sentence. That verifies the intended shared vocabulary and route are being
communicated. The other conditions also communicated correct immediate
work state and boundaries; named stages were not required for their full credit.

### Pickup and the safe blocked case

Published case `227883cfa7c7` preserved all four known business choices, commits,
verification history and a new open question: should a nonempty all-zero catalog
produce `[]` or one zero-count summary object? Its successor cited those records
and asked that same question before implementation. Summary remained absent, so
the no-intervention completion criterion was unmet.

**This is not lost context or dishonest completion.** Recovery, scope, preservation
and truth received full credit. The difference concerns an additional business
edge decision and the degree of autonomous interpretation; the original brief
did not explicitly settle the all-zero output shape. Current and bare workers
selected `[]`. Do not turn this one safe block into a general claim that the old
handoff mechanism is inferior or that asking a material question is undesirable.

### Is selection sufficiently simple?

Every current answer put an appropriate next step first and supplied a usable
request or action. The recommended routes were clarify, build, debug, review then
verification, and verification/pending-runtime handling. No user had to execute
the entire catalog to take that first step.

There is a real presentation tradeoff. Four current answers also displayed the
ten-skill stage overview; the ready-task answer referenced one procedure. Published
answers referenced 1–2 registered procedures; bare answers described a usable
action with or without a known name. The current final replies contained
181–271 whitespace-separated words (median 218), versus 159–203 (median 175) for
0.2.0 and 140–187 (median 155) for no bodies. Ten overview names are not ten equally
required alternatives, but they are more visible information.

Therefore **the next choice was actionable; a reduction in visible information
was not demonstrated**. A concrete follow-up would keep the next recommendation
and copyable request prominent and make the broader map optional after initial
orientation. This is a proposal for later usability testing, not a policy change
made during the experiment or a reason to hide the expert catalog.

Published advice case `cc0486d14776` chose the correct build procedure but asked
for Jira metadata or another branch-naming exception before proceeding, although
the context already supplied a descriptive-branch project convention. The starting
path was accordingly partial, rather than an incorrect feature recommendation.
The judge also mentioned absolute links in the relocated packet; that is not
treated as a product defect because the original project paths were valid.

## Separate comparative assessments

**Current versus 0.2.0:** clarity and consistent decision/scope/truth handling were
equally successful. Current COCO communicated its explicit stage framework and
avoided the old ready-task Jira preparation step. Independent pickup completed
5 versus 4 cases, with the old difference being an honestly preserved open edge
decision. These are specific observed differences, not blanket superiority.
The current overview was more expansive, so simpler presentation cannot be inferred
from having fewer introductory categories alone.

**Current versus no provided bodies:** all four requested operational criteria
were met equally in these samples. The bare writer was also explicitly asked
to prepare a colleague's continuation, and it made useful handoff records. COCO
delivers a predefined shared procedure and vocabulary rather than requiring the
team to rediscover or restate its full method; this experiment verified the tested
implementation, handoff and advice paths, but did not measure how much recurring
human instruction it saves. Files-read and reconstruction effort were not
separately quantified; their raw actions are retained. No effort or ease advantage
is established, nor a unique ability to collaborate.

These results support choosing COCO when the team wants its explicit common
workflow. They do not support telling a team that equivalent outcomes are impossible
without it, or that more paperwork is automatically better collaboration.

## Interpretation alongside the completed collaboration comparison

The separate [collaboration comparison](../collaboration-value/report.md) also
uses candidate `622975e`. Its lifecycle starts with undisclosed owner choices and
adds supplier/batch changes before a fresh pickup; its review family exercises
real billing defects. This v2 handoff starts with the business choices already
supplied, while its selection family asks for advice. Successful agreement
transfer here does not establish complete discovery of missing decisions or
execution of the whole requirements interview.

In that other study all five COCO and all five no-body lifecycles needed an
identity-policy correction. COCO had five confirmed avoidable policy revisions
versus six, and all four completed COCO projects elicited the final zero-count
choice versus three of their four completed baseline partners. Those are narrow
observed control/rework distinctions. The additional actor cost and one COCO
timeout also remain. They do not identify a responsible rule or prove that a
particular procedure's benefit covers its cost.

Its trace audit also finds a loaded-but-unfollowed clarification preview and a
repair final that compares against clarified requirements without explaining the
original unapproved assumption. Accordingly, v2's clarity/consistency counts
remain scoped to its supplied-agreement handoffs; they do not certify universal
adherence to every clarification or reporting rule. An instruction not executed
cannot be declared an executed-and-ineffective mechanism.

Neither study reverses the other's frozen observations. Keep their denominators,
completion rates and costs separate. The other study has no 0.2.0 arm and supplies
no additional current-versus-0.2.0 result. Its unelicited zero-price inclusion
default is distinct from this report's old worker preserving the supplied policy
and asking an additional all-zero output-shape question.

For the [reduction plan](../../plans/collaboration-procedure-reduction.md), preserve
the demonstrated clarity, consistency, usable decision sources, truthful pending
states and fresh pickup while exploring smaller procedures. Keep R5 discovery
repair first, R2 state reuse exploratory, and R1/R3 removal deferred. Add a
separate returning-user presentation experiment; its shorter text must retain a
usable next action, purpose/result, discovery and awareness boundaries. Original/
actual/reason reporting is an existing obligation to verify, not a new tracker.

The [isolated refinement study](../procedure-refinement/report.md) records the
additional temporary-project comparisons. Those sandbox candidates change one
factor each; this documentation task applies none to active or installed skills.

Its 30 original project attempts provide a bounded follow-up. In five R2 pairs,
both the unchanged current procedure and the reuse-wording proposal preserve all
clarity, consistency and pickup items, pass the actual CLI/data guards, and report
original expectation, actual changed policy and its reason in every writer final.
Median actor time changes from 214.572 to 204.210 seconds; the frozen 20% reduction
gate is unmet. This reinforces usable continuation under a changed agreement,
without demonstrating that stronger reuse wording makes the procedure cheaper.

In five explicitly returning-user R7 pairs, both arms give suitable, usable next
steps and preserve manual/advice-only boundaries, including pending browser
acceptance. The current procedure already avoids repeating the introductory
ten-skill table. Proposal replies are longer: median 185 versus 168 words. Keep
the first-use/returning-use distinction, but reject a shorter-text claim for this
wording. First-use learning and human choice difficulty remain unmeasured.

R5 supplies no valid five-pair improvement result: four usable pairs have zero
confirmed policy repairs per arm, one proposal question loses a concrete-choice
item, and the contact pair has an incomplete owner contract relative to its
oracle. That pair is retained and excluded symmetrically, with no replacement.
Discovery repair stays a motivated direction from the earlier study; the tested
wording has not earned adoption. These follow-ups add no 0.2.0 or no-body arm and
do not change this report's original comparative counts.

## Costs and boundaries of the conclusion

| Activity, five samples | Current median seconds [min,max] | 0.2.0 | No bodies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Writer plus successor | 253.684 [218.500,307.147] | 211.132 [199.714,282.626] | 206.042 [166.152,250.538] |
| Advice response | 43.171 [33.836,61.524] | 34.977 [31.920,47.644] | 26.837 [23.007,28.077] |

These are descriptive native process elapsed times, not measured human effort or
causal speed estimates. Other separately frozen local experiments shared the host.
The old handoff cost includes one blocked successor, so it cannot be interpreted
as equivalent completed-work speed. Commands, per-worker usage, cached tokens and
all case values remain in the structured evidence. Additional effort can buy a
desired team deliverable; time alone does not decide its value.

The no-body condition remains catalog-aware: runtime metadata/prior model knowledge
was not removed. Two no-body advice responses named `coco-build` or `coco-debug`
without reading a skill body. Thus the comparison isolates provided procedural
bodies in this environment, not a completely unexposed human or model. All 30
recorded-action audits found only permitted project/guide reads, no external
conversation retrieval, source/control access or publication/global changes;
all provided guide hashes remained unchanged. There were no excluded or unresolved
trials. One old advice context corrected a shell-quoting error within its retained
native turn; no actor was rerun.

The second worker is a fresh model context, **not an actual human teammate**.
Real comprehension, satisfaction, learning time, long-term maintenance and novice
retention were not measured. Five handoffs in one stdlib domain and five distinct
advice contexts support a bounded operational check, not all-project guarantees.
Technical authority was explicit; its persistence and specialist consent were not
inferred from these results. Existing two-arm collaboration experiments were left
unchanged and are not pooled into this v2 three-condition comparison.
