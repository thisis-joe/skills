## Bounds, availability and queue ownership

Each actor call retains 420 seconds; each classifier retains 90 seconds. Each
project retains at most five actor turns, three ordinary owner-answer rounds,
one corrective reply and at most one no-facts continuation within that cap.
Question delivery and actual settlement remain separate. Never treat a delivered
"not decided" as a chosen default. Unanswered material policy questions take
precedence over declaring provisional implementation a ready failure.

A native service probe precedes primary execution. Availability is a point-in-time
observation, not a guarantee for the whole study. At most two projects run at once.
The recovery scheduler admits only a bounded number of active futures instead of
submitting the entire queue. An operational native failure or unresolved owner
instrument failure halts new admissions and writes a stop receipt; active calls
use the existing native engine's bounded cancellation. A cooperative STOP file
allows the owner to halt the queue without suspending a driver or inventing an exit.
Successfully recovered within-cap calls retain their actual outcomes and wait costs.
Known capacity failure does not trigger another automatic attempt immediately.

No timed-out or unsafe project is resumed. No favorable-only replacements and no
budget increases. Infrastructure recovery requiring another cohort must be recorded
and justified separately; unstarted projects are not failures or successful samples.
Keep every original attempt, partial event, owner request/answer, checkpoint,
source manifest, cost and operational limitation. Capacity recurrence is surfaced
early rather than hidden until a nominal completion report.
