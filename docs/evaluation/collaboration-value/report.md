# COCO collaboration value comparison

Status: complete. All twenty attempts and both cold blind audits are retained.
Frozen inputs: candidate `622975e`, driver `4c7ede8`.

## What this comparison can decide

Five paired inventory projects exercise ambiguous business choices, a supplier/
batch scope change, a planned committed stop and a new conversation with no old
transcript. A separate five-pair billing review repeats three actual root defect
types on the same patch within each pair. These are two families of five pairs,
not fifteen independent lifecycle stages or fifteen distinct defect types.

Both conditions receive the same project rules, initial technical authorization,
tools and native default invocation. This is an explicitly automatic profile;
material product choices remain with the owner. COCO loads frozen local `.guide`
bodies; the baseline loads no skill bodies. The experiment measures incremental
effect above a competent agent with project instructions. It does not remove
the base model's knowledge or all inherited catalog metadata, or measure native
installation/discovery usability. Model diversity and separate specialists are
not exercised. See the [frozen protocol](protocol.md) and [scope](../../specs/collaboration-value.md).

## Intervention and rework interpretation

Count delivered synthetic-owner messages separately from scheduled scope/stop/
resume inputs, genuine product choices, factual corrections and procedural
re-authorization. Fewer questions are not automatically better: a compatible
unconfirmed default is different from an owner decision. Compare the four
jointly completed lifecycle pairs separately so that stopping early cannot
appear to reduce owner burden. Human active time, understanding and satisfaction
are unmeasured.

The initial identity assumption needed correction in every lifecycle in both arms.
Source traces show collision and malformed-row choices elicited, but raw SKU
identity first implemented without the owner's trim/case choice. A saved spec
and green local tests did not prevent that missing agreement. These are actual
assumption repairs. Supplier namespace, migration, batch replay and related test
changes are requested scope change, not avoidable rework by definition.

The [all-trace audit](trace-audit.md) finds a narrow observed rework advantage:
COCO has five confirmed product-policy revision episodes, the baseline six.
An episode revises already-implemented behavior to resolve an undisclosed
owner-owned choice, grouping related collision rules into one episode. Baseline
seed 4 implements and commits reject defaults before its first policy question,
then changes file/stored collision handling after the reply, followed by a
separate identity repair. The other nine wait for that first answer before
writing product code. This one extra episode belongs in the comparison; it is
not proof that a particular COCO rule caused its avoidance. Later timed-out work
is unknown, not counted as zero additional rework.

At least one baseline case chose the compatible include-zero default without
eliciting it, while COCO recorded and later asked that unresolved question.
Other baseline cases also recorded unresolved choices and asked on resume.
Neither outcome proves that a previously supplied zero choice was remembered;
new elicitation and memory recovery remain separate observations. A baseline
case re-asks previously supplied grouping/empty-output facts, but its COCO mate
never reaches resume, so that observation cannot establish a matched recovery
advantage. Conditional zero-only-group clarification is not automatically a
forgotten fact merely because a topic label repeats.

All four completed COCO projects elicit the final zero-count choice; three of
the four matched baseline projects do. The remaining baseline default happens
to match the hidden profile but violates the common reservation of material
product choices to the owner. Its passing artifact checks do not establish full
contractual compliance. This is a narrow owner-control distinction, not evidence
of faster work or fewer corrective messages.

The blind owner auditor confirms one genuine public-contract re-ask, one
compatible but never-elicited zero-count default, and no unasked private fact
release. Seven completed change finals contain a zero-count question outside
classifier coverage (four COCO, three baseline). These are unanswered choices
carried into the planned next session, not delivered owner messages or forgotten
answers. The raw classifier tables are therefore not a complete count of question
spans. They do not lose a new distinct private topic in those seven cases, because
the topic is subsequently asked on resume. Blind classifications that label a
conditional zero-only-supplier choice as public-contract repetition are retained
but not credited as actual forgetting.

Git permission/locking retries are operational failures, not skill ceremony.
Repeated baseline and CLI checks also occur without COCO. Identify unchanged
relevant state before calling a check redundant; source churn and additional
tests are descriptive, not automatic waste or quality gains.

## Instrumentation and sensitivity

The entire first attempted primary is excluded and retained under
[invalidation record](evidence/invalid-attempt/invalidation.json). Its controller
loop incorrectly answered scheduled trial questions instead of advancing the
trial. Two lifecycle runs, two completed reviews and sixteen explicitly
not-executed projects remain visible. Both conditions then ran fresh under the
corrected frozen driver. This was a post-exposure repair, not a claim that all
harness defects were caught beforehand.

The fresh cohort also contains a confirmed checkpoint/procedure classification
error for COCO seed 2: "After your trial, what is the next checkpoint?" receives
a generic authorization reply. The induced actor turn takes exactly 9.770s,
with no commands, tool items or file/Git changes; its extra owner classifier
takes 7.733s. Raw totals remain primary. A labeled sensitivity may remove only
that observed exchange, retaining the later identity repair and timeout. This
does not reconstruct all cognitive effects of the extra context. Removing only
9.770 actor seconds changes seed 2 from 759.226 to 749.456, still above its
397.667-second baseline; its timeout and unfinished result remain. The all-attempt
median and five-of-five cost direction are unchanged. Removing its 7.733 owner
seconds yields 215.407 versus 203.998; one extra delivery is instrumentation.
Excluding this pair from completed-work comparison already gives the same four
matched pairs above. None of these adjustments supplies a missing resume.

Abrupt recovery was not demonstrated: the host's pre-primary forced-stop probe
could not prove tool-child cancellation. The primary uses normal native turn
completion at a planned checkpoint, then a genuinely new thread. Unexecuted
summary evidence is unknown, not proof that pending work was resumed.

## What the three originals mean here

The original trees were not experimental arms. Their effectiveness or lack of
effectiveness cannot be inferred from this comparison. The
[pinned source audit](upstream-intent.md) distinguishes design intent and author
experience from independently demonstrated effects:

| Source | Intended mechanism | Material COCO adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Matt Pocock | Developer-controlled composition, dependency-ready grilling, independent Spec/Standards challenge | Focused interview defaults; full frontier grilling on request; review perspectives conditional |
| Addy Osmani | Lifecycle consistency, executable quality gates, durable recovery, actual test-sensitivity checks | Recovery substantially retained; mutation execution conditional on recognized uncertainty |
| Lauren Tan / pstack | Deep design, lead judgment, delegated implementation and model-diverse challenge; optional planning | Model diversity optional; saved spec plus plan mandatory by the user's team decision |

Coverage of the original text does not establish preservation or execution of
its causal mechanism. COCO's support for different runtimes is a legitimate
portability choice with a tradeoff, not proof of equivalent independent challenge.
The mandatory document policy and commit-before-formal-review timing are team
choices. Their costs belong to this combination, not automatically to each
upstream author.

Loaded instructions are also not guaranteed behavior. In COCO seed 0 the actor
read clarification/preview rules, then implemented after the collision/invalid
reply without further-choice preview. Identity was material but not explicitly
listed in the brief. This is discovery/adherence uncertainty, not proof that an
unexecuted preview mechanism is ineffective. The repair's cause appears in its
commentary and plan, but the final report compares only against revised
requirements and says "no material departure". That falls short of the requested
original-expectation comparison; it is a reporting limitation, not a functional
quality deduction.

The hypotheses that integration diluted a useful mechanism, the baseline already
performs the important actions, or overhead outweighs prevention are compatible
with some traces. This two-arm study does not isolate their causal contributions.
Fame, rule count and philosophical appeal do not establish incremental benefit.

## Separate workflow evidence and its implications

The separate workflow-v2 evaluation at source commit
`f4584200cbdc930a7967577c0fc2a97a8d286a50` tests the same candidate `622975e`
against published 0.2.0 and no provided skill bodies. Its 30 temporary projects
and 45 fresh model contexts cover settled-choice handoffs and next-action advice,
not this cohort's unsettled choices, scope changes or defect reviews. The
[reference manifest](evidence/workflow-v2-reference.json) pins the source revision,
file hashes and full native archive identity; the
[reference bundle](evidence/workflow-v2-reference.tar.gz) retains its report,
protocol, specification, case table, results, controls, source audit and final
review. It is a source subset, not another copy of the complete native archive.

| Separate workflow-v2 observation, five cases per condition | COCO | Published 0.2.0 | No provided bodies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clear agreement/state/next action, consistent boundaries and agreement recovery | 5/5 on each applicable axis | 5/5 | 5/5 |
| Fresh-worker pickup completes summary and preserves import | 5/5 | 4/5; one safe block | 5/5 |
| Suitable usable next-action advice without an added prerequisite | 5/5 | 4/5; one Jira preparation condition | 5/5 |

These are achieved shared-procedure outcomes: records were used by a fresh
worker to recover agreement and finish real CLI work, and the advice supplied
a usable next step. A team can require that predefined procedure and vocabulary
as a deliverable without requiring a speed advantage. However, the equally
successful no-body writers were also asked to prepare a colleague's continuation.
Neither unique collaboration ability nor savings in repeated human instruction,
reading, reconstruction or learning were demonstrated. Successors and advice
judges were model contexts, not human teammates or novice participants; the
no-body condition retained catalog awareness.

The old blocked handoff preserved the known choices and correctly asked about
an unresolved all-zero summary shape. Its lower no-intervention completion is
not lost context, dishonest completion or evidence against asking material
questions. This is distinct from this cohort's unasked zero-count owner preference.
Keep truthful uncertainty, owner control and completion as separate observations.

All five COCO advice replies communicated the four-stage framework; four also
showed the ten-skill overview. The next action was usable, but the replies had
218 median whitespace-separated words versus 175 for 0.2.0 and 155 without
bodies. Reduced visible information was not demonstrated; word count does not
establish human comprehension. This motivates testing less repeated orientation
while retaining initial orientation, manual skill awareness and expert discovery.

The primary tables, failures, judgments and frozen reduction gates above remain
unchanged. The two cohorts have different tasks and rubrics: do not pool their
denominators or use workflow success to cancel the observed execution premium,
missing identity decisions or timeout. Conversely, the primary comparison does
not erase the separately demonstrated delivery of a working shared procedure.

## Decision and next work

The result is mixed, with a substantial observed execution premium. There is
one fewer confirmed avoidable policy revision and one fewer unasked final
business choice in the matched completed COCO projects. There is no improvement
in measured identity-correction burden, artifact checks on jointly completed
projects, or seeded review recall; recovery superiority is not demonstrated.
The COCO timeout remains adverse operational evidence. This does not establish
that the small observed benefits justify the added cost, or that they never
would. Human decision value and effort were not measured.

Neither family authorizes automatic removal under the frozen screen. Lifecycle
has five cost increases, but its observed rework gain defeats the required
"improves none" condition. Review has only three cost increases, below four.
The response is targeted redesign and a single-factor follow-up, not declaring
every extra procedure ineffective. The
[procedure-reduction plan](../../plans/collaboration-procedure-reduction.md)
preserves the user's accepted decisions, separates discovery/adherence repairs
from removal candidates, and changes one factor per future ablation. Active
skills are unchanged by this evaluation; no global installation or publication.

Prioritize R5, concrete discovery of behavior-changing choices, as a quality
repair; it must address identity without treating automatic technical execution
as permission to invent business defaults. Then explore R2, reusing the required
spec/plan and linking decisions instead of duplicating state. R2 is an unscreened
efficiency hypothesis, not an approved removal. Keep R1/R3 review-wrapper removal
deferred. Test independent challenge and actual mutation separately on held-out
tasks with room to improve, rather than changing this cohort until COCO wins.

Preserve the separately demonstrated procedure delivery when redesigning R5/R2:
material questions may require a safe stop, and fewer records must still support
agreement recovery and correct pickup. Add R7 as a separate repeated-orientation
usability experiment; it does not alter the R5-then-R2 priority or qualify R1/R3
for removal. Human comprehension and guidance effort remain to be measured.

This comparison does not rerun or replace the earlier
[0.2.0 comparison](../staged-development/report.md). A better workflow than an
older workflow and incremental benefit over a capable no-skill baseline are
different questions; neither result implies the other.

## Limits and closure

There are five pairs per family, with one synthetic owner profile per seed;
these are small local Python/SQLite tasks, not representative production work.
The baseline has strong project instructions. The planned stop is not crash
recovery, and no native installation or first-use learning was tested. Review
repeats three mechanisms rather than sampling fifteen distinct bugs. Actor and
judge default model/runtime/cache variability remain; labels are hidden from
cold judges but style can reveal procedure use. Operational judging prompts were
finalized after exposure using the shared rubric, not preregistered verbatim.
The original trees, specialists and model-diverse challenge were not run. No
statistical equivalence, universal superiority or human satisfaction follows.

Cold review grading costs 95.575s, 155,305 input tokens (137,856 cached) and 3,191
output tokens; owner auditing costs 93.153s, 214,180 inputs (183,296 cached) and
3,499 outputs. These are study costs, separate from actor and synthetic-owner
costs, and exclude design/preflight/invalidated-run expenses. Full native costs
for those earlier runs are retained but no total development bill is claimed.

Required closure: validator passed for 29 skills, source checksums, coverage and
references; [152 tests passed](evidence/primary/closure-tests.log) in 35.891s.
The [freeze check](evidence/primary/freeze-check.json) confirms all eight original
harness hashes and the copied skill snapshot; active skill files differ from
candidate `622975e` in zero paths.
